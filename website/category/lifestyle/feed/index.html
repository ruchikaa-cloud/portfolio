1. Could you introduce yourselves and share where you’re from?

A: I’m Aradhna. I grew up in a few different places partly in the UAE and partly in New Zealand. But most of my childhood was in Bangalore. After finishing 10th grade, I moved to New Zealand by myself and lived with family while studying.

J: And I’m Josh. I’m from New Zealand. I grew up in a small town called Wanganui. I stayed there until I finished school and then moved to Auckland for university. That’s where I met Aradhna.

2. How did your story begin? Where and how did you first meet?

A: We first met casually at university. My best friend was in Josh’s computer science class and she introduced us once in the group study area. We didn’t really talk much after that. A few months went by, and then one day she asked me if I wanted to come hang out with her comp-sci group again and Josh was there. That time, we actually got to know each other more and started spending more time together.

J: Yeah, we didn’t really force anything. It just happened quite naturally.

3. What were your first impressions of each other?

J: I thought Aradhna was really cool and fun to talk to. We realized we had similar interests especially Game of Thrones, which at that time wasn’t something everyone was watching yet. So we bonded over that instantly.

A: Yes! It was definitely the Game of Thrones era.
My first impression of Josh was that he was quiet at first. But when we texted, he would send long, thoughtful, often funny replies. So I realized he had a lot of depth he just opened up slowly in person.

4. When did you realize the relationship was becoming something serious and long-term?

A: Since we met at 19, we didn’t think too far ahead. We just went with the flow.

J: Yeah. There wasn’t a single dramatic moment. We simply kept choosing each other every day. Eventually due to circumstances, we moved in together. Even then, it felt like, “Okay, we’re dating and living together.” And suddenly it had been almost ten years. At that point, it just made sense to get married.

A: It never felt rushed or pressured. It just became home.

5. Coming from different cultures, what similarities did you notice early in the relationship?

A: Both our families are Christian more cultural than strict. So a lot of the values and the way we were brought up were similar. That made things comfortable.

J: Yeah, there weren’t major clashes in belief systems or worldviews. So we didn’t have to navigate intense cultural conflict the way some intercultural couples do.

6. What cultural or lifestyle differences did you have to adapt to?

J: Definitely family lifestyle. My family is very calm and private. We spend time together quietly, watching a movie or playing board games.
Aradhna’s family is energetic, social, and loud in the best possible way. There’s always someone calling, visiting, or staying over. In the beginning it was new for me, because I wasn’t used to that level of activity.

A: Yeah, my family is always on the move—there’s always noise, food, music, plans. When my parents visit New Zealand, even then we’re constantly doing something.
So learning to slow down when we were at Josh’s home felt refreshing. It was something I didn’t realize I needed.

A (continued): Another one was food. Everyone always assumes Josh can’t handle spice. But he can! And he’s probably the least picky eater I’ve ever met. That made life very easy because he adapted to Indian food instantly.

J: I think I just showed up and ate whatever everyone else ate. And it worked out.

7. How did your families react to your intercultural relationship?

J: I wasn’t the first intercultural marriage in my family my uncle is married to a Japanese woman so my family didn’t really find it unusual.

A: My parents weren’t shocked either, but they didn’t love the idea of us moving in together so young. That worried them more than the cultural difference.
But when my mom finally met Josh at my graduation, everything changed. She saw he was kind, grounded, respectful. Then it all felt much more natural.

8. How did you plan the wedding, and did you blend traditions from both cultures?

A: We had our wedding and main celebration in New Zealand, and then a reception in Bangalore so neither side had to travel too far.
One meaningful thing was that during the ceremony, I wore a mangalsutra but I also wanted Josh to wear one. So, we created our own small tradition, where both of us exchanged mangalsutras. It felt symbolic of equal partnership.

J: And splitting the celebrations helped. Weddings in New Zealand are very expensive if you invite hundreds of people. So having the reception in India made it easier to include everyone.

A: Only sad part: Josh’s parents couldn’t attend the Indian reception. But his brother and sister came, and that made it special.

9. What part of the wedding felt most meaningful to you?

A: The meaning came from the time we had already spent together. We had lived with each other’s siblings, visited each other’s families multiple times, shared daily routines. So getting married felt like a continuation of something already strong and warm not a beginning from scratch.

J: This is my fourth time in India now. It doesn’t feel foreign anymore. Our families blended naturally, and our friends became our shared support system. That made the wedding feel grounded.

10. What advice would you give to couples from different cultural backgrounds?

Both: Communicate early. Communicate openly. Communicate honestly.

J: Talk about expectations from both families, possible challenges, and long-term plans before things become serious. Don’t avoid tough conversations.

A: Also discuss practical life decisions where you want to live, careers, family involvement. There may not be perfect answers, but being aware of each other’s realities makes everything easier.

J: And if your families struggle to accept the relationship, build a support system friends can become your chosen family.

A: And remember sometimes your partner’s reactions come from cultural experiences different from yours. Be patient. Listen. Give space. That’s where understanding grows.

There was a time when wedding dessert meant just a three-tier cake and a few small cupcakes. But now, dessert has become a statement piece at a wedding; it is much more meaningful, personal, and heartfelt.

We have come a long way from the buffet system to the world of intimate weddings, now desserts work as storytellers of the couple’s taste and aesthetic. It’s all about refinement as minimalism has stepped into the spotlight.

There is a liking towards beige dessert tables and handwritten note tags for their guests. Rows of macrons, which are engraved with couples’ initials. It’s about how we experience the wedding, and now dessert has become a part of the décor, as tablescapes have intertwined with desserts look and feel.

Sentimental sweets

There is an emotion linked to dessert. Couples want their dessert to speak; they want it to have a memory and gratitude in them. That is why the personalised desserts have become big. We see handwritten notes tied around jars and calligraphed macarons. It’s an experience for guest to walk into a minimalist dessert shelf with their names and immense emotional value attached to each piece.

Desserts have become an art form; couples love to experiment with presentation. From garden cakes of display with outdoor greenery, petals, and soft pastel tomes. We see fondue fountains and doughnut towers. Every setup has a personality to reflect; couples love to show monochromatic displays with sleek jar bars and add a small dessert card.

Desserts in intimate weddings, a new, deeper connection

As weddings have become smaller and it has become more about people, the dessert setups have started reflecting a sense of intimacy. With fewer guests, the focus shifts from quantity to quality, and now it’s all about handcrafted selection for a few.

Each dessert becomes a token of appreciation, a symbol of closeness. A new trend we see is about family-run bakeries and the couples baking the desserts. They now make desserts to infuse warmth and care towards the wedding and celebrations. It is a beautiful trend, the rise of home artisans and bakers who bring their crafts from their own homes. Their work brings on intimate feelings and a raw moment; every swirl of frosting adds a layer of passion and love.

The couples now reach out to small at-home creators for customized desserts that feel and between the maker and the couple, it acts as a strong bond and warmth towards the wedding. It also acts as a form of sustainability, sourcing local ingredients and waste reduction. It focuses more on the time and effort put in by the maker rather than mass production. It’s a conscious effort of celebration.

Designing with emotions: a visual language of love

New modern weddings treat desserts as a design element of love. Every dessert contributes to the overall mood, which then involves the tones, placement, plates, candles, and glass.

The colour palette becomes a hugely important factor for weddings and desserts; it has moved towards subtle elegance, ivory, nude, soft pinks, or powder blues. Edible floral decorations have also become big, as we get to see texture, a gold foil accent brings a sense of celebration without overdoing the setup.

Some couples include their story in the dessert from “how we met” to “our favourites”. Like a tiramisu shot can showcase their first official date, a gelato bar could show their trip to Italy, a doughnut bar could showcase how both of them love doughnuts, a shared love.

Designing small cards that talk about how much it means to them for the guest to be a part of their big day. It is used to create moments and to show the power of gratitude. It’s a gesture that showcases the desserts as more than just a sweet dish. It’s more than just a sugar rush; it’s a memory that they get to take home as a sense of keepsake.

Minimalism meets emotions and the journey

A trend that blends beautifully with love and weddings. The harmony of minimalism serves as a glimpse of the intimate moments between the couples. Couples are trying to make it more personal and real with their guests; there are sentiments attached to their desserts, and meaningful, beautiful desserts crafted accordingly.

With desserts, we are redefining the purpose of food in weddings. We see desserts as exchanging love and happiness with each other rather than just a show of. Desserts are a beautiful way to showcase the story of two worlds coming together and the sweetness shared with loved ones.

A wedding table is more than just a place for the guests to sit, but it is where all the love stories unfold. There is laughter, there are connections, and there are emotions which add to the entire celebration. Modern couples now treat table settings as an expression of what their marriage is all about, and so it is a way of an individual story. From floral centrepieces to Mediterranean themes, every little detail adds to the tale of romance.

Romantic Glow, Florals, Candles and Warm Textures

There is nothing as beautiful as a floral centrepiece lit up with candles. It’s a classic combination which adds warmth and a sense of intimate glow to the table. Adding fairy lights to the candles can create a dreamy ambience. Another element that we could add through textures is wooden and rustic tables, which give out an earthy tonality and raw textures to the table. These can be best paired for outdoor weddings with tall candles and dried-up flowers to give a sense of height and drama to the table setting, and account as timeless elegance for the table.

Vintage and Mediterranean Dreams

Though both are far apart, the new trends emerge as charming and beautiful. The vintage trend is used by couples who adore old-world romance. Elements like lace runners and antique cutlery or menus with wax seals on them add to the whole nostalgic feeling, and it becomes sophisticated and heartfelt for the guests.
On the other hand, Mediterranean-inspired tablescapes are best for destination weddings because they give off a breezy contrast with blue hues, terracotta tones, and a coastal charm. They all come together to show how traditions can inspire aesthetics and can come together in beautiful wedding décor.

Personal Touches

Personalization is a huge element of table settings as it becomes the essence of true luxury. Engraved table names and handwritten menus with wax seals on them give a sense of belonging and show how much effort the couples have put in. Draped fabrics on the floor show motion and movement and enhance the overall aesthetic. Another new trend of ceramics has come in where matte green and blacks have made a comeback, and it makes us feel artisanal and grounded with earthy tones. It’s perfect for couples who love minimalism and a handcrafted touch to their wedding.

A Palette of Love

Colour-themed settings bring everything together; it becomes cohesive. From napkins to florals, every shade has the couple’s vision in it. Current trends start from soft ivory and nude shades to the trend of matte black and gold accents, which are used to show a vibrant contrast for the evening. A pop of colour makes everything come alive through tinted glassware or plates, if it has a colour pop, it makes everything lively.
Pampas grass continues to be the favourite of many modern-day couples as it becomes a statement piece. Its soft textures bring out effortlessness and sophistication, and when it’s combined with fairy-lit glass jars which are suspended in the air, it creates a visual fairytale that feels ethereal.

Modern Flow Coming Together

Long tables have been a couple’s favourite in the entirety of the wedding ceremony because they give a sense of togetherness, allowing conversations and moments to be shared naturally. The newest trend is serpentine or curved tables, which add motion and give a contemporary twist to the entire layout. Both styles are loved and celebrate connection and truly talk about what weddings are really about.

Beauty as it Meets Emotion

A wedding table isn’t just décor anymore it’s an emotion of celebration. Whether you want rustic simplicity, vintage charm, modern luxury, or just the arrangement of simple candles and floral décor, in the end, what matters is when it all comes together and is perfectly placed. That is what true table placement is all about.
