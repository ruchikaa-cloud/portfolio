Why are the 2025 trends not as ethical as they say?


The fashion industry is constantly evolving and changing but not every trend is as progressive. Some trends dominate social media and the spaces others are overlooked, but here are some of the most problematic trends of 2025 that need your attention.


Number 1.

Hyper youthful aesthetics in adult fashion

This basically means while we are trying to be played full and nostalgic getting back the elements from the past there is an obsession with looking younger.

We see this in all the major industries whether it’s the music industry, film industry or the fashion industry. Because of the high beauty standards and the obsession with looking younger it’s getting into a very unsettling space.


Getting the soft girl and childlike aesthetic: we get to see a lot of baby teas is with cartoon graphics coming into play which my make the childhood styles. It’s gotten a sense of a youthful desire and getting trends like the coquette Trend, has shown to have a big diversion and it’s to show a new sense of humility which is innocent and non-threatening.

They are trying to reinforce the soft girl image but to an extent where its imaginary.

In fashion industries its professional to embrace all stages of life rather than constantly promoting one aesthetic which is youthful and stronger the ultimate deal.


Number 2.

Fast luxury:  a fake – false promise

The new wave of multiple brands emerging as fast luxury shows their trying to mimic fast fashion through high craftsmanship and keeping the prices relatively low. This upcoming trend has some serious down sides to it.


Exploitation: consumers can’t really see them from the naked eye but the luxurious market relies on spoiled labour practices. The people who are working in this industry’s face unfair wages and poor working conditions.

Low quality disguised as luxury: many brands use low quality materials but price it as very high prices. What people have noticed is its sensitive illusion that the premium industry is trying to showcase but in reality, the durability is as low.


Number 3

Plagiarism and AI generated fast fashion

AI is everywhere it’s fully embedded and functional in the fashion industries the truth about this is that now designer and brands no longer rely on human creativity.

AI generates around 34 million designs per minute and the algorithms respond to the trends of today. Although an invention but the production and the creativity of consumers mind is taking serious consequences to this.


Artist exploitation: many tools come out of the internet for inspiration, which leads the designers to be blatant and have no thoughts about their very own designs. The basic philosophy that AI uses is that it read the entire internet and then mimics the patterns of designers, then twist and turns it into an Ai generated design. The reality of this is even though AI has a sped-up version to this but it doesn’t mean it’s always a good thing, more clothes are produced and discarded to an unsustainable rate. This then leads to struggles with landfill overflow. The fashion industry is losing it touch with the natural art form and now they rely on AI.


Number 4

The new unrealistic workplace aesthetic

We can’t forget the 2025 corporate fashion which is coming up. It’s all about power suits and office wear with Bizarre and unrealistic aesthetics to it. It doesn’t mean to generalize the entire corporate fashion but, there is something that should be spoken about which is the hyper sexualise corporate wear.

We get to see share blouses and blazers which are worn with overly short mini dresses, but because of places like the tik Tok and Instagram fashion trends which is the CEO chic they have become more and more unrealistic and a sense of fantasy.

This is basically influencers showing a glamorous and absurd version of a corporate life for the employees, where they tend to show thigh high boots with corset tops which do not actually go with the aesthetic of a corporate where and it disconnect them from the reality of their consumers and the people who go to those jobs on a daily basis.

There is a pressure on women for them to balance the way they dress and nobody really speaks about it. This is when they have to create a very neat balance between being sexy and being formal there always under the pressure of getting objectified in their environment and then redirect them from what they are working for in a professional environment.

Number 5

Festival fashion 2.0

The influencer market now dictates that every festival, every occasion has to have a new outfit. It pushes the consumer to get carried away from the trend, therefore leading to buying of unnecessary items which are only a one-time use.

Themed clothing: Mass production in low quality, it is leading to massive textile waste.

Fun fact: festival outfits are mostly made from plastic heavy materials contributing immensely to pollution.

In conclusion, the fashion industry presents itself as an ethical to and inclusive industry but beneath all of this is a surface which people haven’t seen which is different from the reality. They highlight how the industries till priorities their gains and profits over being true to the consumer.
